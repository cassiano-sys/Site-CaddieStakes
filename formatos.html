How handicap factors in

All three formats compare net scores, not gross — that's what lets players of different skill levels compete on even footing.

  • Every hole on the course has a handicap (stroke) index from 1 to 18, hardest to easiest
  • The higher a player's handicap, the earlier they get extra strokes, starting on the hardest holes
  • Net score on a hole = strokes taken − strokes received on that hole
Good to know: the app does this math for you automatically, hole by hole, the moment you enter the score — you just type in what actually happened on the shot.
Format 1
01

Nassau

Three bets in a single round: Front 9 (holes 1–9), Back 9 (10–18) and Total 18 — each with its own value (app default: R$20 / R$20 / R$40).

  • Each hole decides 1 point: the lower net score gets +1, the other gets −1; a tie is 0
  • Points add up within each segment (Front and Back are tracked separately)
  • At the end, whoever's positive in a segment collects that segment's value; whoever's negative pays it
The Press, in action

Whenever a bet's running score reaches ±2 points (individual match) or ±4 points (team match), a Press is automatically born — a new side bet, same stakes, starting fresh from that hole through the end of the segment. It gives whoever's losing a chance to claw back, and more than one Press can cascade within the same Front or Back.

Each player can still choose whether they also count toward the individual head-to-head matchups inside the team, without affecting the team bet itself.
Format 2
02

Match Play

It's the exact Nassau you just read about — Front 9 / Back 9 / Total 18, same values, same hole-by-hole scoring — just without the Press. The running score plays straight through from the first to the last hole of each segment, with no side bet spawning midway.

  • Same team and individual-matchup structure as Nassau
  • Up 2, 5, or 8 points — doesn't matter: no Press ever fires, it's the same single bet from start to finish of the segment
When to pick it: if the group prefers a more predictable match — without the Press's snowball effect stacking bets late in the Front or Back — Match Play is Nassau in "classic mode."
Format 3
03

Catraca

Nassau turned all the way up: instead of waiting for a ±2-point gap (individual play) before a Press fires, here it only takes 1 point — the score "ratchets" forward every hole you're up. In team play, the Press interval stays the same as regular Nassau (±4 points).

  • In individual play, any 1-point lead already fires a new Press — one can cascade from every following hole
  • The opening Front 9 and Back 9 bet (before any Press) already starts worth double each Press's value — Total 18 has no Press and is worth exactly what you enter, never doubled
Example with Front 9 = $5 / Back 9 = $5 / Total 18 = $10

Each Press is worth $5 (the entered value). But the nine itself — Front 9 and Back 9 — already starts worth $10 each, double the Press, even before the first Press fires. Total 18 stays at $10, exactly as entered, with no Press and no doubling.

When to pick it: for groups who find regular Nassau too slow to turn around — in Catraca, a run of 2 or 3 bad holes already stacks up several Presses, so the score changes hands fast.
Format 4
04

Medal

Pure stroke play: Front 9, Back 9 and Total 18 are three independent prizes (same values as Nassau) — whoever has the lowest net-stroke total in that segment takes it.

  • Everyone competes together, not in pairs — there's no H2H, just one combined total per segment
  • A full tie on a segment: nobody wins or pays (a push)
  • Whoever didn't win that segment splits its value and pays the winner
Good to know: since everyone adds up all 18 holes (not points per hole like Nassau), Medal rewards consistency — one extra stroke on any hole lands straight on the segment total.
Format 5
05

Skins

Every hole is worth 1 skin. Whoever has the sole lowest net score on that hole takes it.

  • A tie on the hole: nobody wins it — the skin carries over to the next one
  • Several holes in a row can stack up until someone finally wins one outright
  • Final payout = accumulated skins × value per skin (default: R$20) — whoever won zero skins splits the total payout among themselves

Format 6
06

Stableford

Points, not strokes — one bad hole only costs you that hole's point, it doesn't wreck the rest of the round.

  • Add up the points from all 18 holes — whoever has the most points at the end wins
  • Money reflects the point difference between the leader and the runner-up, multiplied by the value per point (default: R$20) — whoever didn't lead splits that amount among themselves
Good to know: it's the most forgiving format on an off day — a stray "10" is worth exactly the same (0 points) as a "7", so it never sinks the round.
